01Strategic Discovery

Questions that diagnose before they pitch

The arc never changes: understand the process → find the friction → establish urgency → earn the next step. Open with a proof point tied to their exact pressure, then ask. Control of the conversation comes from better questions, not a better demo.

The core sevenRun this in order

  • Process: Walk me through how care coordination works today — rounding, discharge, post-discharge follow-up.
  • Friction: Where does it tend to break down or get hard to do consistently?
  • Friction: How much still relies on manual work, staff memory, or individual consistency?
  • Friction: Are you focused mainly on high-risk patients today, or trying to cover broader populations?
  • Urgency: What's getting the most leadership attention — readmissions, staffing, throughput, experience, reimbursement, TEAM?
  • Urgency: Is there a specific initiative or mandate driving this conversation?
  • Next step: It sounds like there's opportunity around [pain] — does it make sense to bring in a workflow specialist to show how peers approach it?

Challenger layerReframe & teach

  • “Most systems can document care. Far fewer can execute it. Where does intent turn into action breaking down for you?”
  • “What does a missed post-discharge follow-up actually cost you — in readmission risk, in penalty exposure, in staff time?”
  • “If you looked across every facility, would the workflow execute the same way? What varies?”
  • “Where would reducing administrative burden create the biggest clinical impact?”
  • “What's the cost of waiting on the EHR roadmap versus solving this now?”

Ask the question that matches the seat

CNO / CNIO — “How confident are you that every discharged patient gets the intended follow-up — and how would you know if they didn't?”

CFO — “Where are you carrying penalty exposure or leaving TCM / appointment revenue on the table today?”

CIO — “How much IT lift do new patient-communication programs take today, and how does that compete with your roadmap?”

CMO — “How are you closing care gaps and confirming discharge comprehension across the medical staff?”

COO — “Where do throughput and service recovery slow down because issues surface too late?”

CEO — “Three years out, what has to be true about how this system coordinates care — and what's blocking it now?”

The 2026 GTM solution plays — TCM, TEAM, ED PDO

For 2026 we lead with three programs — each clears the bar of clear economic value, proven outcomes, minimal net-new lift, and strong differentiation vs. the EHR. Quality & Safety stays a strong supplemental play. Cheat sheet — TCM: CPT 99495 / 99496, 2-business-day contact + med rec, ~$200+/patient. All three lead plays run a Day 2 / 9 / 16 & 23 / 30 cadence with HL7/Flowsheet integration and a 4–8 week implementation.

TCM — Transitional Care ManagementDriver: readmission risk + unbilled TCM revenue›

What it is. A reimbursable Medicare service for the 30 days after discharge. CPT 99495 (face-to-face visit within 14 days) and 99496 (within 7 days) both require interactive contact within 2 business days of discharge plus medication reconciliation. Done right it cuts readmissions and captures revenue most systems leave on the table.

Why it matters now

Every eligible discharge that misses the 2-business-day contact is both a readmission risk and unbilled revenue — and few systems hit that window manually at scale. (Rates are set annually by the Medicare PFS; keep dollars approximate.)

How we run it

Automated outreach fires inside the 2-business-day window on every qualifying discharge, runs the check-in and med-rec prompts, transfers anyone who needs a clinician to a live nurse, and schedules the 7- or 14-day visit — building the documented contact trail billing requires.

Proof

Intermountain $15M saved & 41% readmission drop; reach 96% of patients vs. ~40% on the portal; 250 clinical hours back per 1,000 discharges.

Discovery

“What share of discharges get a live touch within two business days?” · “Are you billing TCM — and if not, what's blocking the 2-day contact and med-rec documentation?”

Objection

“Our clinics already call.” → At what reach and with what documentation? We automate the 2-day contact, capture the med-rec trail, and route only the patients who need a nurse.

Cadence & integration

Five touches — Day 2 (status, Rx obtained, fall risk, SDOH), Day 9, Day 16 & 23, Day 30 (program satisfaction). Inbound HL7-ADT; outbound HL7-ORU to Epic Flowsheets + HL7-MDM. 4–8 week implementation. Assets in flight: webpage, sell sheet, case study, reference video, ROI calculator, discovery guide.

TEAM — Transforming Episode Accountability ModelDriver: CMS mandatory bundle, 2026–2030›

What it is. CMS's mandatory, episode-based payment model — ~700 acute-care hospitals across 188 CBSAs, running 2026–2030. For Original Medicare patients undergoing one of five surgical procedures (joint replacement, hip/femur fracture, spinal fusion, CABG, major bowel), the hospital is accountable for cost and quality from surgery through 30 days post-discharge, including SNF, home health, and follow-up. It also requires connecting patients to primary care.

Why it matters now

If a hospital is in a mandatory CBSA, the 30-day post-surgical window is now a P&L line. One avoidable readmission in a TEAM episode costs ~$14,000; episode spend runs ~$28K (joint) to ~$50K (CABG).

How we run it

We own the 30-day episode follow-up: condition-specific check-ins on the surgical cohorts, escalation and live nurse transfer when recovery slips, SNF/home-health transition support, and the required primary-care connection.

Proof

Post-discharge programs cutting readmissions 32–41% (Intermountain, Community Health Network); 3.4× lower readmission likelihood with automated follow-up (Sentara).

Discovery

“Are any of your CBSAs in the mandatory TEAM cohort?” · “Which of the five episodes is highest-volume?” · “How are you managing the 30-day window and the primary-care connection today?”

Objection

“Our surgeons and post-acute team handle recovery.” → TEAM holds the hospital accountable across settings you don't control. We give you eyes on the patient for the full 30 days.

Cadence & integration

Same Day 2 / 9 / 16 & 23 / 30 rhythm, tuned to surgical recovery (pain, surgical site, PT, physician handoff). Episode-specific topics for LEJR, SHFFT, spinal fusion, CABG, and major bowel. No mandated script. Eligibility via HL7 or flat file; results flow back to Epic via Flowsheet. 4–8 week implementation.

ED Outreach — Post-Emergency-Department Follow-UpDriver: avoidable ED returns + high-risk capture›

What it is. Structured follow-up 24–72 hours after an ED discharge. Unlike inpatient, an ED discharge usually has no scheduled follow-up and no reliable PCP handoff — driving avoidable return visits and admissions. An avoidable-utilization and high-risk-capture play that also surfaces social barriers.

Why it matters now

The ED is the front door for avoidable utilization. A 72-hour bounce-back is costly and often preventable with one timely contact — and many of these patients never reach primary care.

How we run it

Automated outreach within 24–72 hours screens for red-flag symptoms and aftercare comprehension, confirms the prescription was filled, books PCP/specialist follow-up, screens for SDOH, and transfers anyone deteriorating to a live nurse.

Proof

~$2 per automated call vs. ~$82 manual (UCSF) makes ED-volume outreach affordable; reach 96% vs. ~40% portal; +35-point LTR at Prisma's largest ED over two years.

Discovery

“What happens in the 72 hours after an ED discharge today?” · “What's your 72-hour ED return rate, and do you know who didn't follow up?”

Objection

“ED patients are one-and-done.” → No follow-up is exactly why they come back. One automated touch catches deterioration and routes the high-risk few to a nurse.

Story-led — the Margaret move

Margaret, 72: ED for chest pain, discharged with 3 Rx + a cardiology follow-up, back in 2 days — couldn't afford 2 Rx, didn't know the follow-up was required. She's your 38%. Outcomes: +45 NPS · +22.7 nurse communication · 30%↓ readmits · ~$7K per prevented return, ~$3.6M/yr. Three questions that sell it: “What happens in the first 72 hours after they leave?” · “How do you know if a patient didn't understand discharge instructions?” · “What would it mean to intervene before the bounce-back?” The line: “We're not replacing what your team does — we're protecting it.”

Quality & Safety Bundle — supplemental playDriver: HAC Reduction Program 1% penalty›

What it is. Proactive, purposeful patient and location rounding to catch risk before it becomes a harm event — CAUTI, CLABSI, falls (especially with injury), and pressure injuries (HAPI). Tied to the CMS HAC Reduction Program (1% Medicare cut for the worst quartile), Value-Based Purchasing, and Leapfrog.

Why it matters now

HACs are both a safety failure and a direct penalty, plus reputational exposure through Leapfrog and Star ratings. Paper rounding is inconsistent and surfaces issues too late.

How we run it

Digital patient and location rounding standardizes the safety checks (line necessity, skin, fall risk, environment), routes any negative signal to the right owner in minutes, and uses ambient listening to keep documentation off the nurse.

Proof

ChristianaCare 41% / 12% / 20% HAI / HAPI / Falls and 80+ zero-harm awards; Mid-Atlantic 78% / 85% falls / falls-with-injury; Northern VA 30%+ CAUTI & CLABSI.

Discovery

“Where do you stand on the HAC Reduction Program — at risk of the 1% penalty?” · “How consistent is safety rounding across units, and how fast does a flag reach the right person?”

Objection

“We already round.” → On paper, inconsistently? Digital rounding makes it standard across every unit and routes risks for real-time recovery.

04Ideal Customer Profile

Who we win with — and who we don't

We sell to health systems where coordination at scale is a board-level pressure. The strongest fits feel the pain across multiple facilities and have an executive owner for an outcome we move.

FirmographicsThe fit

  • U.S. health systems & hospitals — NAICS 622110 (General Medical & Surgical Hospitals), not the broad “Healthcare Services” filter.
  • Epic-first is a fit, not a blocker — 70%+ of customers / 350+ hospitals run Epic.
  • Multi-facility systems, children's hospitals, rural/FQHC, post-acute, physician groups, and increasingly ACOs / CINs / MSSP participants.
  • An executive owner for readmissions, HCAHPS, staffing, throughput, access, or TCM/revenue capture.

Buying patternLand & expand

  • Typical new land ~$75K (single workflow), enterprise deals $250–400K bolting programs together.
  • Sales cycle ~9–12 months; multi-threaded across clinical, IT, finance, and experience.
  • Start on the most acute pain, expand on proven outcomes (Outreach-only and Rounding-only bases are prime cross-sell).

Watch-outsWeaker fit

  • Single small clinics with no scale pain and no executive owner.
  • Pure self-service buyers unwilling to staff or sponsor a program.
  • Accounts where no measurable outcome is on the line this fiscal year.

CRM noteFix the ACO relic

ACO/CIN legal entities of existing hospital customers may be tagged “Does Not Meet ICP” or “Do Not Contact” from the old ICP. That's now a target, not a disqualifier — flag these for correction before prospecting.

Multi-thread rule

Never single-thread a deal

The clinical champion (CNO/CNIO) opens the door, the CFO funds it, the CIO de-risks it, and the CEO/COO sponsors the transformation. Map all seats early — the Strategic Deal tracker's four imperatives keep you honest on who's missing.

Open-ended questions that get each seat talking, each anchored to a metric so the cost of the status quo surfaces in their own words. The full questioning discipline lives in Strategic Discovery (01) and Challenger Selling (08).

CFO · revenue & penalties

“With HRRP penalties common and ~$15,200 per avoidable readmission, what's your annual exposure if post-discharge engagement is inconsistent?”

PX Leader · HCAHPS & equity

“HCAHPS care-transition scores affect VBP — if few patients get follow-up, how will you close the communication gaps and improve star ratings?”

09Epic & EHR Integration

Integration questions show up in almost every deal, usually from a CIO or integration analyst, and often as the gate between a clinical yes and a signed contract. Three Epic integration capabilities

A growing set of innovations delivers expanded services from one unified platform — and secures us as a pillar of the patient-engagement program, not a point tool that's easy to rip out.

Flowsheets · the 2026 headline

Maps outreach-response data directly to discrete Flowsheet fields in the Epic chart, in near-real-time. The only one of the three that also lets the team respond to patients faster with highly reliable deliverability.

EHR Activation Gateway

Streamlines how programs are activated and how patient data moves between CipherHealth and Epic. Lead with it when the buyer wants to consolidate vendors.

Enhanced Appt Reminders

Two-way reminders with rescheduling written back to the EHR scheduling workflow. Your access, no-show, and revenue conversation.

What a Flowsheet is — and the before/after

A Flowsheet is one of Epic's most-used tools: a structured grid in the patient's chart where discrete data is recorded over time, manipulated, and reported on. Because results land as discrete fields — not a PDF or free-text note — clinicians can act and analysts can trend.

The old workflow

Care teams viewed call responses in one tool (Evolve) and everything else — meds, independent calls — in Epic. Data reached parts of the team as much as 24 hours later via a nightly batch.

The new workflow

Responses flow into Epic for a unified view in near-real-time. Inbound demographics via HL7-ADT; results written to Flowsheets within seconds. Siloed, day-late data is now in the chart the moment it's captured.

“Near-real-time,” said precisely: to the human eye it's real time — messages flow within seconds. We say near-real-time only to account for the seconds a message takes to reach the EMR. It is not a nightly batch; that was the old way.

How the data flows

Inbound — eligibility

HL7-ADT carries demographics and encounter info to enroll eligible patients; a nightly SFTP flat file is the fallback where no live ADT feed exists.

Outbound — results

HL7-ORU writes discrete results to Epic Flowsheets in near-real-time; HL7-MDM can push patient-issue documentation into the chart.

What does NOT flow to Epic

Only automated call results, in raw data format. Analytics — reach rate, site/program breakdowns, time-to-callback — stay in CipherHealth dashboards. Set this expectation early.

Why our approach wins

Flowsheets is a bi-directional feed that's configurable without deploying new code — so we implement it for more clients, faster. Similar bespoke feeds (Intermountain/Cerner, Anmed/Epic) needed custom engineering; ours doesn't.

Selling it — scope, fit, objections

Implementation & scope

~3–8 weeks of scoping, build, and testing for a standard post-discharge program. Build size scales with the number of questions (Flowsheet rows ∝ program questions). Penn's first program took ~8 weeks; with steady resourcing they now go live every 3–4 weeks.

Where it fits

Best: programs with yes/no or structured answer types — clean mapping to discrete rows. Not a fit: free-text fields. Most popular: post-discharge results into the EMR.

Multi-instance / Cerner

Single-program multi-Epic-instance isn't supported today (active partnership work). Flowsheets is primarily Epic; other EMRs use different integration types — submit a Product DAR to scope, don't guess in the room.

Discovery openers

“Where does your team see post-discharge call results today — chart or separate tool?” · “How long after a patient responds does the team see it?” · “Which programs are mostly yes/no? Those are the cleanest Flowsheets candidates.”

11Competing with Epic

Epic is the system of record. We're the system of action.

“Why not just use Epic?” shows up in most deals. This is your battlecard — the competitive companion to Module 09 (how we plug into Epic). Lead with the structural argument, not a feature fight, then prove it with labor economics. Epic documents and bills; it's a passive worklist that still needs a nurse to act. We're the active agent that secures the contact, runs the check-in, and escalates only when a human is needed.

Why this fight matters in 2026 — the labor-vs-leakage paradox

To capture the revenue they're owed and avoid the penalties they now face, systems would have to hire people they don't have. Epic-native worklists add manual work; we automate it. Name the paradox, then show the math.

TCM

~$259 per eligible patient ($220 moderate / $298 high) across 10M+ Medicare discharges — and most systems miss 30–60%. A missed 48-hour contact ≈ $250K lost per 1,000 discharges.

TEAM

Mandatory for ~741 hospitals, no opt-out; an unplanned readmission costs ~$14K–$30K. Epic logs the surgery but doesn't catch the Day-3 red flag.

Post-ED

A ~$38B annual drain; ~20% return within 30 days at $1,200–$2,500 each. Epic records the return after the patient is back.

The program teardown — passive vs. active

TCM

Epic: manual worklist — no answer, no revenue. Cipher: automates the CMS interactive contact, documents to the flowsheet, secures the ~$298 manual workflows miss.

TEAM

Epic: logs the surgery; doesn't prevent the complication. Cipher: a digital safety net — automated symptom checks catch red flags around Day 3, before a ~$30K readmission.

ED

Epic: records the return after arrival. Cipher: a virtual triage — spots medication confusion and routes to pharmacy before they return.

The proof — labor arbitrage at Advocate Health

The labor-reduction engine

Advocate runs its outreach program with 17 FTEs vs. ~41 manually — a 59% staffing reduction. Roughly $3.08M → $1.28M: about $1.8M saved a year and ~2.4× ROI on staffing alone.

The CFO reframe

We don't add a tool — we remove labor you can't hire for. When a buyer assumes Epic-native is cheaper “because it's already paid for,” the license is sunk but the labor to run a passive worklist is not — and that labor is the scarce resource.

Epic vs. CipherHealth — the structural comparison

Epic — System of Record

Holds clinical data for documentation and billing · portal-based, MyChart-login reach · limited, manual triage · rigid, IT-heavy to change · no published post-discharge outcomes.

CipherHealth — System of Action

Condition-specific interaction that drives outcomes · digital-first, no login, hard-to-reach populations · automated triage at population scale · fast, low-IT deployment · dozens of peer-reviewed published outcomes.

Objection handling

“Epic can already do this.”

Epic can record it; it can't run it. It's a passive worklist that still needs a nurse on every call. Same program: 17 FTEs with us vs. ~41 manually.
